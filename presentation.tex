\begin{frame}{The customer need}
\begin{itemize}
\item A bounded project may need several kinds of expertise.
\item Customers still need scoping, coordination and a responsible contact.
\item AI assistance adds capacity; acceptance still needs judgment.
\end{itemize}
\Sources{Context: \SourceLink. Its engineer range is \Money{120,000}--\Money{165,000}; only 16 of 551 engineer postings disclosed pay. Salary and project prices cover different work.}
\note{The commercial source supplies context rather than proof of demand for this platform. Its arithmetic has an inconsistent midpoint; no reported all-in total is reused. The whitepaper contains the source URL and review date.}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{The project constellation}
{\fontsize{30}{38}\selectfont One customer-facing lead, flexible specialist capacity.}\par\vspace{5mm}
\begin{itemize}
\item Human operators manage on-platform chatbot conversations.
\item Freelancers subcontract bounded tasks to other freelancers.
\item AI assistants support the team under named human supervision.
\end{itemize}
\note{Team composition can change per milestone. The lead remains accountable under the prime contract; subcontractors are accountable for their own accepted scope. This is the original proposed operating model.}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{Human-operated chatbots}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clarify the request and agree a brief.
\item Bring in specialists when the project needs them.
\item Review uncertain AI work and handle exceptions.
\item Explain progress, cost and the next customer decision.
\end{itemize}
\note{A customer can identify the operator and request human attention. AI may draft messages and summaries; material commitments and spending changes require authorized human approval.}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{The support automation example}
{\fontsize{30}{38}\selectfont A fictional customer asks for routine answers and human escalation.}\par\vspace{5mm}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Operator scopes the workflow and budget.
\item Integration and content specialists join.
\item AI assistants support implementation and tests.
\item Reviewer checks evidence; customer accepts.
\end{enumerate}
\note{This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer case study. The deliverables include configuration, approved knowledge, escalation behavior, test evidence and operating instructions.}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{Contracts and subcontracting}
\begin{itemize}
\item Prime contract: customer outcome and accountable lead.
\item Subcontracts: explicit scope, budget and delegated access.
\item Agent assignments: named human owners and approval gates.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{4mm}{\fontsize{30}{38}\selectfont New scope needs agreement. Completed runs need review.}
\note{Authorized specialists may propose further subcontracting within prime-contract rules. Changes are versioned. Customer acceptance authority does not transfer silently down the chain. Cancellation and disputes pause the affected settlement path.}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{Tenant workspaces and Tether}
\begin{itemize}
\item Tenant-scoped tasks, artifacts, credentials and costs.
\item Tether-bound sessions proposed for AI supervision.
\item Human approvals and agent events in one project record.
\end{itemize}
\Sources{Integration candidate: maintainer's Tether/repofleet README, reviewed 1 November 2026. Adapter compatibility and tenancy are unverified.}
\note{The proposed model connects a task to a supervised session with correlated progress and approval events. Begin with two synthetic tenants. The full multitenant integration is not implemented by this publication.}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{Credits, payments and completion}
\begin{itemize}
\item Estimate and cap API usage before execution.
\item Separate usage credits from reserved contract funds.
\item Review artifacts, checks and revision history.
\item Settle accepted work once; reconcile unused allowances.
\end{itemize}
\note{Execution success, quality review, customer acceptance and payout are separate states. Demonstrate retry, cancellation, budget exhaustion and duplicate-event handling before live billing. Provider secrets remain server-side.}
\end{frame}
